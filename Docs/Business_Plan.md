# Business Plan
## B2B FMCG Marketplace App

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Draft

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The Platform is a B2B e-commerce marketplace connecting small independent retailers (shops, cafes, restaurants) directly with FMCG suppliers and wholesalers. Modeled on the traditional-trade digitization approach pioneered by companies like Cartona, the platform digitizes an inefficient, fragmented supply chain — replacing manual phone-based ordering and cash-only transactions with a mobile-first ordering experience, real-time inventory visibility, and embedded financing (BNPL).

The platform operates an **asset-light marketplace model**: it does not hold inventory itself but earns revenue by facilitating transactions between suppliers/wholesalers and retailers, and by monetizing embedded financial services and value-added data/marketing tools.

## 2. Market Opportunity

### 2.1 Problem
- Traditional trade (independent small retailers) represents the majority of FMCG sales volume in many emerging markets, yet remains largely offline.
- Retailers rely on informal wholesalers, inconsistent pricing, manual ordering (phone calls, in-person visits), and cash payments.
- Suppliers/wholesalers lack visibility into real-time demand, resulting in inefficient distribution and missed sales opportunities.
- Retailers often lack access to credit/financing to manage cash flow for inventory purchases.

- **TAM**: Total FMCG trade volume flowing through traditional trade channels in target country/region
- **SAM**: Trade volume in urban/semi-urban areas with smartphone + delivery infrastructure

### 2.3 Target Segments
1. **Retailers**: independent grocery stores, mini-markets, cafes, restaurants, kiosks
2. **Suppliers**: FMCG manufacturers/brands seeking distribution reach
3. **Wholesalers/Distributors**: existing distribution businesses seeking digital tools and demand aggregation

## 3. Business Model

### 3.1 Revenue Streams
| Stream | Description |
|---|---|
| **Transaction commission** | % fee on GMV (Gross Merchandise Value) charged to suppliers/wholesalers per completed order |
| **Delivery/logistics fee** | Fee for facilitating or providing delivery (if platform-managed logistics) |
| **BNPL financing margin** | Interest/fee margin on retailer credit lines, either in-house or via revenue-share with fintech partner |
| **Advertising & promotions** | Suppliers pay for featured placement, sponsored listings, promotional campaigns |
| **Data & analytics subscription** | Premium analytics/insights tier for suppliers (demand forecasting, market intelligence) |
| **SaaS/ERP tools** | Subscription fee for wholesaler inventory management tools (upsell beyond free tier) |

### 3.2 Asset-Light Model
The platform does not own inventory or (initially) a delivery fleet. It empowers existing wholesalers and suppliers rather than disintermediating them — reducing capital intensity and enabling faster geographic scaling.

## 4. Competitive Landscape

| Competitor Type | Examples | Differentiation Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Direct B2B marketplace competitors | Cartona (Egypt), MaxAB (Egypt), Sabi (Nigeria), Wasoko (East Africa) | Localize deeply: language, payment methods, last-mile logistics partnerships |
| Traditional wholesalers (offline) | Informal local distributors | Win on convenience, price transparency, credit access |
| Vertical B2B players | Category-specific platforms (e.g., pharma, beverages) | Consider category focus first if full FMCG breadth is too capital-intensive initially |

**Key differentiators to build:**
- Deepest retailer trust (reliability, fair pricing, fast delivery)
- Best BNPL/credit terms for retailers
- Strongest supplier data/insights tools
- Localized language & payment support

## 5. Go-to-Market Strategy

### 5.1 Phase 1 — Single City Pilot
- Onboard a small set of suppliers/wholesalers with strong product-category coverage (e.g., beverages, packaged food, personal care)
- Use a field sales agent team to onboard retailers door-to-door (critical in traditional trade — digital-only acquisition rarely works at this stage)
- Offer promotional pricing/incentives for first orders to drive habit formation

### 5.2 Phase 2 — Density & Retention
- Focus on order frequency and retention in the pilot city before expanding
- Introduce BNPL to increase order size and loyalty
- Build supplier trust through reliable payouts and sales data

### 5.3 Phase 3 — Geographic Expansion
- Replicate playbook city-by-city, prioritizing markets with dense retailer populations and weak existing distribution
- Localize per-market payment and delivery infrastructure

### 5.4 Customer Acquisition Channels
- Field sales agents (primary — high-touch, necessary for trust-building with traditional retailers)
- Referral incentives (retailer-to-retailer)
- Supplier-driven onboarding (suppliers bring their existing retailer relationships onto the platform)

## 6. Operations

### 6.1 Key Operational Functions
- **Field sales & retailer onboarding**
- **Supplier/wholesaler account management**
- **Order operations & dispute resolution**
- **Logistics coordination** (own fleet, wholesaler fleet, or 3rd-party partners)
- **Credit risk management** (for BNPL)
- **Customer support** (multi-channel: in-app, phone, agent-assisted — important given target user comfort with tech)

### 6.2 Key Partnerships
- Fintech/BNPL provider (unless building in-house lending)
- Payment gateway/mobile wallet providers
- 3rd-party logistics providers (for cities without sufficient wholesaler delivery capacity)
- FMCG brand partnerships (anchor suppliers to bootstrap catalog breadth)

## 7. Financial Plan (Framework)

### 7.1 Key Assumptions to Model
- Average Order Value (AOV)
- Order frequency per retailer per month
- Take rate (commission %) per transaction
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC) per retailer (largely driven by field agent cost)
- Retailer retention/churn rate
- BNPL default rate (if applicable)

### 7.2 Cost Structure
| Category | Notes |
|---|---|
| Technology (dev team, infra) | Core cost in early stage |
| Field sales team | Salaries + incentives for retailer/supplier onboarding |
| Logistics | Either owned fleet cost or 3rd-party fees |
| Customer support | Call center / in-app support staff |
| Marketing | Retailer incentives, referral programs |
| BNPL capital/credit risk | Cost of capital + expected default losses |
| G&A | Standard overhead |

### 7.3 Funding Requirements (Framework)
- **Seed/Pre-seed**: Build MVP, validate single-city pilot, prove core ordering loop retention
- **Series A**: Scale to multiple cities, launch BNPL, build out supplier tooling
- **Series B+**: National/regional expansion, deepen financial services

*(Reference: Cartona itself raised an $8.1M Series A extension — useful benchmark for capital intensity of this model at scale.)*

## 8. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Low retailer digital literacy / trust | Heavy field-agent-assisted onboarding; simple UX; SMS fallback for critical alerts |
| Supplier/wholesaler channel conflict (disintermediation fears) | Position as demand-aggregation partner, not replacement — "asset-light, empower not disrupt" positioning |
| BNPL credit risk/defaults | Start conservative credit limits, use order history as underwriting signal, consider partnering with fintech to share risk |
| Logistics reliability in emerging infrastructure | Hybrid model — leverage wholesaler fleets first, add 3rd-party/owned fleet selectively where gaps exist |
| Competitive pressure from well-funded regional players | Focus on a defensible initial geography/category niche before broad expansion |
| Regulatory (financial services, data) | Early legal counsel on BNPL/lending regulations per target market |
